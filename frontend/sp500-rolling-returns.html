Rolling Returns

See how the length of time you hold an investment changed the range of outcomes. For every starting year since 1928, this shows what the following 1, 3, 5, 7 and 10 years historically returned. Short holding periods produced a very wide spread of results; longer ones narrowed it considerably. Figures come from the Damodaran dataset at NYU Stern.

Each rolling return is the geometric mean (CAGR) of that year's return and the N−1 years that followed it. A cell shows — when the dataset doesn't yet have enough future years to complete that window.
