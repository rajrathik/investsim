💼 Asset Allocation Simulator — How It Works

What It Does

  • Simulates investing a monthly amount (optionally growing each year) across your chosen asset mix
  • Uses actual historical prices from Yahoo Finance — not projections or averages
  • Buys whole shares only at the monthly high price (conservative, worst-case entry)
  • If allocation can't yet buy a share, funds accumulate in a per-ticker bucket until they can
  • Dividends are tracked as cash received — not reinvested back into equities
  • Cash dividends earn money market interest at the historical FRED federal funds rate

How to Use It

  1. Set your monthly amount, optional annual deposit growth, and investment period (1–20 years)
  2. Pick tickers from the dropdown — search by symbol or name, or browse the list
  3. Set allocation % using sliders — total must equal 100%
  4. Click ▶ Run Simulation to see results

What You'll See

  • 6 summary tiles — Total Invested, Equity Value, Dividends Received, Cash Accrual, Portfolio Balance, Money Market Benchmark
  • Interactive charts — hover for month-by-month growth and dividend details
  • Monthly breakdown table — click any highlighted cell for per-ticker detail
  • Tax impact section — adjust your tax rate to see yearly liability on dividends and interest
  • Annual returns — pre-tax performance per calendar year, with clickable detail modals

Key Things to Know

  • All summary values are clickable — cards, table cells, and chart points reveal detailed breakdowns
  • If an asset wasn't tradeable in a given month, its budget share automatically flows to the other assets that month
  • The MMF column shows what the same total invested would have earned in a money market fund — a risk-free benchmark
  • The Annual Deposit Growth field adds a flat dollar amount each year: Year 1 = base, Year 2 = base + growth, Year 3 = base + 2× growth, etc.

How Share Accumulation Works — Example

Setup: $1,000/month split 60% Holding A ($250/share) and 40% Holding B ($800/share)

Month 1: $600 → Holding A bucket, $400 → Holding B bucket
Holding A: buy 2 shares ($500), carry forward $100
Holding B: $400 not enough yet, accumulates in bucket

Month 2: $600 + $100 carry = $700 for A → buy 2 shares ($500), carry $200
$400 + $400 carry = $800 for B → buy 1 share, carry $0

If a holding is unavailable: its budget automatically shifts to the other holdings that month. Normal split resumes next month.

📈 Sector Analysis Tools — How to Use Them

Sectors Covered

  • XLK — Technology  |  XLV — Health Care  |  XLF — Financials
  • XLE — Energy  |  XLY — Consumer Discretionary  |  XLP — Consumer Staples
  • XLI — Industrials  |  XLB — Materials  |  XLU — Utilities
  • XLC — Communication Services  |  XLRE — Real Estate  |  VTI — Total Market

Reading the Data

  • Total Return = (price change + dividends) ÷ year-begin price. Dividends are counted as cash, not reinvested.
  • CAGR — Compound Annual Growth Rate over the selected period
  • Correlation — +1.0 means sectors move in lockstep; 0 = independent; −1.0 = opposite
  • Drawdown — Peak-to-trough % loss from the highest point before a decline
  • All tools have year range controls — adjust to focus on any time window

Building a Diversified Portfolio

  • Use Sector Performance to identify sectors with strong long-term total returns
  • Use Correlation to find sectors that don't move together — combining them reduces volatility
  • Use Drawdown to understand the worst-case loss you'd have experienced
  • Use Risk vs Return to spot efficient sectors (high return, low volatility)
  • Then use the Asset Allocation Simulator to test your chosen mix with real history
