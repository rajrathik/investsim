Welcome to the Asset Allocation Simulator

A quick guide before you start

📈

What This Tool Does

  • Simulates investing a monthly amount (optionally growing each year) across asset mix
  • Uses historical prices from Yahoo Finance — not projections
  • Buys whole shares at the monthly high price (worst-case entry)
  • If allocation can't buy a share, it accumulates in that ticker's bucket until it can
  • Tracks dividends as cash — not reinvested into equities
🎯

How to Use It

  1. Set your monthly amount, optional annual growth, and investment period (1–20 years)
  2. Pick tickers from the dropdown — search or browse
  3. Set allocation % using sliders — must total 100%
  4. Click ▶ Run Simulation
📊

What You'll See

  • 6 summary tiles — Invested, Equity Value, Dividends, Cash Accrual, Balance, Money Market benchmark
  • Interactive charts — hover for month-by-month growth and dividend details
  • Monthly breakdown table — click any highlighted cell for per-ticker detail
  • Tax impact section — adjust tax rate to see yearly liability on dividends and interest
  • Annual returns — pre-tax performance per calendar year
💡

Key Things to Know

  • All summary values are clickable — cards, table cells, and chart points reveal detailed breakdowns
  • If an asset wasn't available to invest in a given month, its share of the budget automatically flows to the other assets that month
  • The MMF column shows what the same money would earn in a money market fund — a risk-free benchmark
🧮

How Money Is Invested — A Simple Example

Say you invest $1,000/month split 60% Holding A and 40% Holding B.

Each month: $600 goes into Holding A's bucket, $400 into Holding B's bucket.

Buying shares: If Holding A costs $250/share, your $600 buys 2 shares ($500) and the leftover $100 stays in the bucket. Next month, $600 more arrives → bucket has $700 → buys 2 more shares, $200 carries forward. Your money is accumulated and waits until there's enough for the next whole share.

If a holding isn't available: Suppose Holding B wasn't available to invest — in that case that $400 automatically shifts to Holding A for that month — so your full $1,000 stays at work. Next month, when both are available, the normal 60/40 split resumes.
