How to read this

Higher correlation means two investments have historically moved more similarly. Lower correlation may provide greater diversification.

Pairs with low correlation (below 0.5) have historically moved less in step with each other, so a decline in one has not reliably coincided with a decline in the other.

Technology (XLK) and Utilities (XLU) are an example of a historically low-correlation pair, compared with two sectors that both track technology.

Worth noting: The number of holdings and the correlation between them are separate things — a portfolio can hold many sectors that have historically moved together.
